TriSonica Wind Logger

Connect the anemometer. Connect power. Check the green light.

LI-COR LI-550 · Raspberry Pi 3B+
Records to SD card · GPS time & position
No keyboard · No screen · No commands

Starting a measurement

STEP 1 Anemometer Plug the TriSonica into any USB port on the Pi.
STEP 2 GPS to the sky Point the small square antenna upward, with a clear view. It will not work indoors.
STEP 3 Power Connect power and wait about 20 seconds, then read the green light.

The green light — count the flashes, then the pause

One flash Ready
Nothing to do. Recording, and the clock is confirmed by GPS.
Two flashes Time not confirmed
Give it sky and wait. Recording is fine, but the times are not yet trustworthy. Can take 15 minutes from cold.
Three flashes No anemometer
Check the USB cable at both ends. Nothing is being recorded.
Fast flicker Bad readings
Check the sensor head. See overleaf. Recording continues and every bad reading is marked.
Mostly on Card full
Stop and contact the technician. Nothing new is being saved. Data already recorded is safe.
Even blink Copying to USB
Do not remove the stick until this returns to one flash.

If the light flickers fast

  1. Look into the gap between the ultrasonic heads. It must be completely clear — a spider web or a leaf is enough.
  2. Move it away from surfaces. It needs 3–5 m of clear space from walls, vehicles and trees.
  3. Stand it upright and level.
  4. Check the power — if a powered USB hub is used, confirm it is switched on.

Getting the data out

  1. Plug a USB stick into the Pi while it is running.
  2. The light changes to an even blink while it copies.
  3. When it returns to one flash, remove the stick.
  4. The stick holds a dated folder plus EXPORT_STATUS.txt, which confirms every file was verified.

Nothing is deleted from the Pi. The data stays on it as well.

Stopping · and the two things that matter

To stop, simply unplug the power. Nothing needs to be shut down first — at most the last few seconds of data are lost.

Never unplug the small GPS wires from the pin header, and avoid knocking them. Four fine wires carry it, and moving one to the wrong pin can permanently damage the computer.
The red light must stay on, steady. If it goes dark or flickers, the power supply is not strong enough and the measurements will be wrong.
Data is written to the SD card continuously and flushed every 5 seconds. Card holds ~31 days at 10 Hz
